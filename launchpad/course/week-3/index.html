Course · Week 3 of 8

Define the solution

Decide the smallest thing that solves the sharpest part of the problem.

What you hand in this week

A one-line pitch, the core features, and a clear list of what you will not build.

Why this week matters

Your first product should be the smallest thing that fixes the sharpest moment from your interviews, and this talk is about exactly that kind of scoping.

1. Watch and read

How to Build a "Minimum Viable Product", Michael Seibel, Y Combinator.

  • How to Build a "Minimum Viable Product" ↗
    Michael Seibel, Y Combinator · Video
    Use it to write your core features and your not-building list.

2. Do the work

  1. Reread your week 2 notes and mark the moment that hurt the most.
  2. Write the one-line pitch using the template and test it on two people.
  3. List three to five core features and a separate list of what you will not build.
  4. Write down the single belief that, if wrong, would kill the idea.

3. Use the template

One-line pitch and scope template. Copy it, fill it in, then enter your answers on your dashboard.

One-liner: For [who] who [problem], [product] is a [category] that [benefit], unlike [what they use today]. Core features (3 to 5): 1. 2. 3. Not building yet: - - - Riskiest assumption: [If this is wrong, the idea does not work] How I will test it: [What I will see if it is true]

4. What a pass looks like

Your submission is checked against this list. Meet every line and the week is passed.

  • The one-line pitch names who it is for, the problem, and what is different from today's alternative
  • Lists between three and five core features
  • Lists what will deliberately not be built
  • Names a specific riskiest assumption that can be tested
  • The solution clearly follows from the problem and the conversations in week 2

Common mistakes

  • Listing fifteen features because they all seem useful.
  • A one-liner full of words like 'platform', 'ecosystem' or 'AI-powered' with no clear benefit.
  • Skipping the not-building list, which is where scope creep begins.
