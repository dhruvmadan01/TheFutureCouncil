Course · Week 7 of 8

Pitch deck

Tell the story of your startup in ten slides and three minutes.

What you hand in this week

An 8 to 10 slide deck and a 3-minute recorded pitch.

Why this week matters

You now have real evidence, so this week is about telling the story clearly in ten slides and three minutes.

1. Watch and read

How to Pitch Your Startup, Kevin Hale, Y Combinator.

  • How to Pitch Your Startup ↗
    Kevin Hale, Y Combinator · Video
    Structure your deck and script around it.
  • Startup Business Models and Pricing ↗
    Aaron Epstein, Y Combinator · Video
    Helps with the business model slide.

2. Do the work

  1. Draft the ten slides using the structure and your real numbers from weeks 2 to 6.
  2. Write a 400-word script and trim it to fit three minutes.
  3. Pitch to your pod, listen to what they remember, and revise.
  4. Record the final three-minute pitch and share both links.

3. Use the template

Three-minute pitch script template. Copy it, fill it in, then hand in your answers in the app.

Opening (20 sec): [The problem, in a way anyone understands] What I built (30 sec): Proof (70 sec): [Conversations, users, return numbers, one strong quote] What I learned (20 sec): Why me and why now (20 sec): The ask (20 sec): [One clear, specific request]

4. What a pass looks like

Your submission is checked against this list. Meet every line and the week is passed.

  • A deck link is provided that anyone with the link can open
  • The deck covers problem, solution, product, traction, team and an ask, in 8 to 10 slides
  • A recorded pitch link is provided and is intended to run about three minutes
  • Traction in the deck uses real numbers from the earlier milestones
  • The ask is specific rather than a general request for support

Common mistakes

  • Spending the pitch on market size and almost nothing on evidence.
  • A vague ask like 'we are looking for support'.
  • Reading the slides aloud instead of telling the story.
Passing this week unlocks: Eligibility for the Demo Day ranking.
