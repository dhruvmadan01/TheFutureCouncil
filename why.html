The builder manifesto

There is a better way to spend four years.

We were told the plan was simple. Crack the exam, get the seat, clear the placement season, and life is set.

The plan is breaking. Offers are harder to come by. Machines are learning the work that entry-level jobs were made of. A degree on its own proves less every year.

Build something. Find a problem you cannot stop thinking about, find the people who care about it too, and ship.

You might fail. Most startups do. You will still leave college having made something real, for real people, with real stakes. That should be normal in every college, in every city. That is why The Future Council exists.

Placement prep vs. building

What you actually learn.

Customers, not puzzles

Placement prep: leetcode puzzles with predetermined answers. Building: 20+ user interviews and learning what people will pay for.

Shipping, not rehearsing

Placement prep: mock interviews where nothing breaks. Building: live software, real users and iteration from feedback.

Revenue, not rubrics

Placement prep: answers fitted to a rubric someone else wrote. Building: convincing strangers to try your product and collecting your first rupee.

Proof of work

You graduate having founded a team, built software and pitched investors. You are hired for what you shipped.

Institutional support and policy

The safety nets you did not know existed.

You do not have to choose between a degree and building a startup. Many institutions and national policy already protect student founders.

Deferred placement

Several IITs and IIMs formally allow graduating students who start companies to defer campus placement for up to two years. If your venture does not work out, you return to the placement drive.

NISP 2019

The National Innovation and Startup Policy, framed by the Ministry of Education and AICTE, asks universities to allow semester and 1-year gap breaks, course credits for startup prototypes, and attendance relaxation for student founders. Read the policy ↗ Ask your dean or T&P cell for these rights.

For Indian parents

A letter to parents.

Dear parents,

We know how hard you worked to get your child into college. You want them to have financial security, dignity and a clear path ahead.

When your child says they want to build a startup, it is natural to worry. But building in college is no longer a reckless gamble. It is the fastest way to become exceptionally capable.

How The Future Council protects your child:

  • Founder-first terms. Chapters and community membership are free with zero equity. For the selective Launchpad Fellowship we take 2% equity in the 20 selected ventures, founders keep 98%, and there is no success fee on funds raised. Applying costs a one-time ₹999.
  • Safety nets exist. Many colleges let students defer placements for up to two years. Check your college's policy.
  • Real skills. Even if the startup fails, companies recruit founders because they know how to build, talk to customers and solve problems alone.

College is the cheapest time in life to take an ambitious swing. Thank you for supporting their agency.

Dhruv Madan & Aryaveer Chauhan · Founders, The Future Council
