The case for building

Don't wait for permission. Build.

The fastest way to learn what a company needs is to start one. Here is what building teaches you, and how the TFC Incubator backs early-stage founders while they do it.

What building teaches you

Skills you only get by doing the work, whether or not the first idea survives.

Talking to customers

You learn what people will actually pay for, which is rarely what you first assumed.

Shipping

A working product in front of real users teaches more than any plan on paper.

Selling

Every founder sells: to customers, to teammates and to investors.

Raising capital

You learn how grants, angels and venture capital work by going through the process.

Making decisions

With limited time and money, you learn to prioritise and to move without perfect information.

Resilience

Most startups hit walls. The founders who learn from them build the next company better.

The Builder Manifesto

Don't queue. Build.

Start before you feel ready

Readiness comes from building, not from waiting. The first version is allowed to be rough.

A product beats a plan

Live prototypes, user conversations and early traction count for more than a polished deck.

Nobody builds alone

Grants, investors, mentors and other founders make the early days shorter and less lonely.

Two ways to spend the next year

The skills are different. So is the proof you end up with.

Waiting for the right moment

  • Planning in the abstract
  • Seeking approval first
  • No real stakes
  • Waiting for permission

Building a startup

  • Talking to real customers
  • Shipping working products
  • Selling and distribution
  • Proof of work
